## The problem we're attacking

Calorie-tracking apps that work from a single photo — Cal AI and its imitators — have
to **guess how much food is on the plate**. That guess is their dominant source of
error. A photo cannot tell a 90 g portion of couscous from a 160 g one, and granular
foods like rice only ever show their top surface.

Time-of-flight sensors and single-image volume estimation don't fix it either: they
smooth over granular foods and still only see the top of a pile.

<Aside type="tip" title="The whole idea, in one sentence">
Stop estimating the portion. **Weigh it.** The camera is demoted to answering only
what the dish *is* — which is the part a camera is actually good at.
</Aside>

## What it is

<CardGrid stagger>
  <Card title="Two ESP32 nodes" icon="seti:cpp">
    **Node A** carries the load cell, the screen and the buttons. **Node B** carries
    the camera. They talk over ESP-NOW, which needs no router.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A backend that does the arithmetic" icon="seti:python">
    Identifies the dish, looks up nutrients per 100 g, multiplies by the **measured**
    mass, stores the meal.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A phone app" icon="device-mobile">
    Daily log, macro totals, history, and a manual-entry fallback for when the
    recogniser says it doesn't know.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Our own food table" icon="open-book">
    Couscous, lablabi, brik, ojja and mloukhia are in **no** food database on earth.
    So we built one.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

Hardware has been requested from the faculty and is **not in hand**. Everything built
so far is therefore testable without it: the backend runs offline, the firmware
compiles with no board attached, and Node A can fake its own weight readings.

<Aside type="caution" title="Read this before quoting any nutrient number">
Every row in our Tunisian food table is an **estimate** derived from recipe
composition — not a lab measurement, because no published composition table exists
for these dishes. Each row carries its derivation and a confidence rating. See
[the food table](/smart-food-scale/deliverables/food-table/).
</Aside>
