# Why these parts

The [component request](/project/component-request) is the sheet handed to the lab.
This page is the working behind it: where the prices come from, what changed and why,
how precise the scale can be, and what to reach for if the plan can't be followed.

<Callout type="note" title="Where the figures come from">

Prices were read from Tunisian shop pages on **12 October 2026** (Didactico, Tuni Smart,
CoThings, Tunisianet) and are written the way those shops write them: 33,500 TND is
thirty-three and a half dinars. Prices change and stock runs out, so check each link before ordering.
"Borrow" is based on what teaching kits and lab suppliers ship, not on this faculty's
inventory: ask the lab technician first, and buy only what they can't hand over.

</Callout>

If the budget allows, add one spare ESP32-CAM (33,500 TND) and one spare load cell
(13,000 TND). They are the two parts most likely to be damaged: the camera board by a
brownout, the cell by someone leaning on the platform.

## What changed from the first draft

The first draft was rebuilt from the project brief. Checking it against datasheets and
against what can be found locally changed these lines.

| Line | First draft | Now | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Programmer | USB-to-TTL adapter (CP2102 / FTDI) | ESP32-CAM-MB base | Cheaper (9,500 vs 10,500 TND), has BOOT and RESET buttons, and plugs straight in. The adapter stays as a backup. |
| Lighting | WS2812B ring, SN74AHCT125 level shifter, 330 Ω | 6 white LEDs behind a diffuser, one 2N2222 | No Tunisian shop we checked stocks the 74AHCT125. The ring's data line needs 3.5 V at 5 V and the ESP32 only guarantees 2.6 V. White LEDs and a 2N2222 are in every lab. |
| Who drives the light | Node A, GPIO 5 (wiring page) / Node B (architecture page) | Node B, GPIO 13 | The two pages disagreed. The camera node lights its own photo. GPIO 13 is free with the microSD in 1-bit mode and is not a strapping pin. |
| HX711 supply | Its own 5 V → 3.3 V buck converter | The DevKit's 3V3 pin, with 10 µF and 100 nF | It draws about 5 mA with the cell. The DevKit's linear regulator is already quieter than a switching converter, and at 3.3 V the data pin stays within what the ESP32 accepts. |
| microSD | Required, 4–8 GB | Borrowed, optional | Any spare card up to 32 GB works. The scale runs without one; it just can't hold captures while Wi-Fi is down. |
| Buzzer | 2N2222 + 1 kΩ | Same, plus a 1N4148 across the coil | Kit buzzers are coils, and a coil switched off by a transistor needs a diode. |
| Load-cell wiring | Not listed | Shielded 4-core cable | The cell's own leads are thin and short. An old USB cable is the right cable. |
| Calibration masses | Optional | Booked from the physics lab | No local shop sells them, and the accuracy figures can't be checked without them. |
| Load cell | 1 kg | 3 kg (YZC-131) | A ceramic plate alone can weigh 300–430 g; with a large meal that passes 1 kg. No 2 kg cell is stocked locally — see [how precise it is](#how-precise-is-it). |

## Check when the parts arrive

1. **Read the DevKit's module marking.** WROOM-32 is what the pin map assumes. A WROVER uses
   GPIO 16 and 17 for its memory, so the HX711 moves to 25 and 26 —
   [wiring](/project/wiring).
2. **Measure the load cell** before buying screws or drilling. Sellers list 75 to 80 mm and
   M4 for the 3 kg YZC-131; batches vary, and the [3D rig](/rig) is drawn from the seller's figure.
3. **On the HX711, check E− is connected to GND**, and measure E+ to E− once powered.
   Many cheap boards leave E− floating, and the readings wander.
4. **Check the arrow on the cell** points the way the load pushes, and that the platform
   touches nothing but the cell.
5. **Refocus the camera lens** at the real mounting height, on a printed target. The
   [3D rig](/rig) places the lens 320 mm above the plate, which leaves margin around an
   18 cm plate.

## How precise is it?

A load cell's errors are a fixed fraction of its **full scale**, so a bigger cell is
coarser in grams. The trade is headroom against resolution:

| Cell | Room for food on a 420 g ceramic plate | Readable, with averaging | Absolute, spec | In Tunisia |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 kg | about 480 g | 0.1–0.2 g | 0.5–1 g | 8,000 TND, Tuni Smart |
| 2 kg | about 1.5 kg | 0.2–0.4 g | 1–2 g | Not stocked by the shops we checked |
| **3 kg** | **about 2.5 kg** | **0.3–0.6 g** | **1.5–3 g** | **13,000 TND, Didactico, in stock** |

"Readable" is 0.01–0.02 % of full scale and "absolute" 0.05–0.1 %, the same rule the
[accuracy page](/reference/accuracy) uses; the plate column allows 100 g for the platform.

**Why 3 kg is still precise enough.** Even the worst case, 3 g on a 350 g portion of
lamb couscous, is 5 kcal out of 585. The nutrient values in the food table are
estimates with far wider error than that. The camera and the table limit this system;
at 3 kg, the scale does not.

**What actually makes it precise**, more than the cell's rating:

- **Calibrate at five points** (100 g, 200 g, 500 g, 1 kg, 2 kg) and correct between them,
  rather than one mass and a straight line. That removes most of the non-linearity, the
  largest term in the spec.
- **Tare immediately before every capture**, which cancels zero drift and creep.
- **Mount the cell properly**: one end on the base, the other under the platform, a gap
  on both sides, the arrow pointing the way the load pushes, and a stop screw under the
  platform so nobody can press it past its limit.
- **Ten minutes' warm-up, 10 samples/s, median of 12.**

Done this way, aim for about 1 g. The [accuracy page](/reference/accuracy) is where the
measured figure goes; report that, not the target.

A 2 kg cell would cut the error by a third, at the cost of an import and a few weeks.

## The light, on Node B

The one circuit that is new in this revision. Six LEDs in parallel, each with its own
resistor, switched on the low side:

| From | Through | To |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPIO 13 | 470 Ω | 2N2222 base |
| 2N2222 base | 10 kΩ | GND, so the light stays off while the board boots |
| 5 V | 100 Ω, then a white LED (×6, in parallel) | 2N2222 collector |
| 2N2222 emitter | — | GND |

About 18 mA per LED, 110 mA in total, drawn from the 5 V supply rather than through the
ESP32-CAM. The firmware switches it on, waits for the camera's exposure to settle, takes
the frame and switches it off. The on-board flash LED on GPIO 4 stays as a fallback, not
the main light: it is a single point beside the lens and glares on a glazed plate.
