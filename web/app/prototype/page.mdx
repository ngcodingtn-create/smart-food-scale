# The prototype, simulated

The scale, the camera, the backend and the phone, working together in one page, before
any of the hardware exists. Put a plate down, tare it, serve a dish, press the button,
and watch the meal arrive on the phone.

<Simulator />

## How to drive it

1. **Put an empty plate on the scale.** The display shows the plate's own mass.
2. **Press TARE.** The display goes to zero. From here on it shows food only.
3. **Choose a dish and a portion, and put the food on.** The reading settles on the portion.
4. **Press PHOTO.** Node A takes twelve readings and keeps the median, hands the grams
   to Node B, Node B lights the plate and takes one frame, and the two travel to the
   backend together.
5. **Look at the phone.** The meal is in today's log, with its mass, its calories, and
   a note that the nutrient values are estimates.

Open **On the wire** under the pipeline to see what each stage actually sent: the twelve
samples, the request, and the backend's JSON reply.

## Things worth breaking

The interesting behaviour of a measuring instrument is what it does when it is used
badly. Each of these is a mistake a real user will make.

| Try this | What happens | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skip TARE, then capture | The plate's mass is logged as food | Nothing downstream can tell. The backend receives a plausible number. |
| Press TARE with food already on | The reading drops to zero and the backend refuses it | Its 422 names the cause: not tared, or the plate was lifted. |
| Untick **Recogniser knows the dish** | The backend answers 422 and lists the dishes it does know | "I don't know" is a real answer. The mass is still good, so the meal can be added by hand. |
| Untick **Wi-Fi reachable** | Node B keeps the frame and the grams on its microSD card | Tick it again and the buffered capture is posted. |
| Take the plate off after taring | A negative reading | The same 422 as an untared scale: the backend cannot tell them apart, and says so. |

## What is real here, and what is not

A simulation that looks finished is easy to mistake for a product. This one is exact
about some things and invented about others, and the two should not be confused.

| Piece | In this simulation | In the project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arithmetic, limits, error wording, JSON shapes | The backend's own, re-implemented line for line | **Built and tested** — 145 tests |
| The 18 dishes and their nutrient values | Copied from the backend's food table by a script | **Built.** Every value is an estimate, flagged as one |
| The reading: 12 samples at 10 SPS, median | The documented configuration, with a little noise added | Planned. No hardware has been measured |
| Capture order: weigh, hand over, photograph, upload | As documented | Firmware **not written** beyond its build file |
| The ESP-NOW message | A stand-in | Packet format not designed yet |
| The photograph | Does not exist. Node B's view is a diagram | No camera in hand |
| Recognising the dish | Told what is on the plate | **Not built.** The backend ships a stub that says it is a stub |
| The phone app | Follows the four planned screens | **Not started** |

<Callout type="caution" title="The recognition step is the one to be sceptical of">

Here the recogniser is correct whenever its box is ticked, because it is handed the
answer. Nothing in this page says how often a real vision model will name a plate of
mloukhia correctly. That number does not exist yet, and the simulation reports no
confidence value rather than inventing one.

## What building this exposed

Wiring every stage to the next, even in a browser, turned up three things the written
plan had not settled.

**Nothing tells the phone that a capture failed.** When the backend cannot name the
dish it answers the camera node, not the app. The plan says the app then offers manual
entry, but the app has no way to know there is anything to enter. In the simulation the
person reads the mass off the display and types it into the app, which is what would
have to happen today. A small "pending capture" record on the backend, holding the mass
until someone names the dish, would close the gap.

**The plate has to be tared empty, before the food.** The architecture page describes
the capture as "tare, then weigh". Read literally, with a loaded plate, that zeroes the
meal away. The order that works is: empty plate, tare, add food, capture. The firmware
should not tare as part of a capture at all.

**The plate spends the cell's range.** A 1 kg cell carrying a 420 g ceramic plate had
580 g left for food, and a large portion went past it. That is why the request now
specifies a 3 kg cell: see [how precise it is](/project/component-request#how-precise-is-it).
Node A still refuses anything past 3 kg, but no plate and portion in this simulation
reach it.
