<details className="rig-more">
  <summary>The assembly, described</summary>
  <div className="rig-assembly" id="rig-assembly-description">
    <p>The <strong>plate</strong> sits on a round platform. The platform is screwed to a riser on the free end of a <strong>3 kg bar load cell</strong>; the cell’s other end is screwed to a spacer on the base. Nothing else touches the platform, so all of the weight passes through the cell. A stop screw under the free end stands 1 mm short of it, so a hand on the platform cannot over-bend the cell.</p>
    <p>Inside the base, an <strong>HX711 amplifier</strong> reads the cell and hands a number to the <strong>ESP32 DevKit V1</strong>. The front edge carries the <strong>OLED display</strong> and two buttons, tare and photo. A buzzer confirms each press. Tare the empty plate, add the food, then press photo.</p>
    <p>An <strong>arm</strong> stands on the base behind the plate and reaches over it. At its tip an <strong>ESP32-CAM</strong> looks straight down, 320 mm above the plate, with <strong>six white LEDs</strong> around the lens behind a diffuser. The base carries the arm; the platform never does.</p>
    <p>The DevKit sends the grams to the camera board over ESP-NOW, with no router between them. The camera board uploads the photo and the grams over Wi-Fi.</p>
  </div>

<details className="rig-more" id="assembly-assumptions">
  <summary>What is assumed</summary>
  <p>The model is drawn in millimetres at one scale. No part is enlarged to make it easier to see. Turning and zooming change the view, not the proportions.</p>
  <p>The base, platform, arm, spacers, stop screw, LED carrier and diffuser are <strong>proposals</strong>, sized to fit the electronics and make the load path clear. They are not a fabrication drawing, and no hardware has been measured. The load cell’s size is a seller’s figure, not a datasheet’s.</p>
  <p>The exploded view moves parts apart without resizing them; its gaps are not dimensions. The light in the model is an illustration, not a lighting simulation. The camera’s field of view is worked out from a quoted 66°; measure it on the real lens.</p>
  <p>The reasoning is in decisions D10 to D13 of the repository’s <code>docs/decisions.md</code>. For the electrical side, see the <a href="/workbench/wire">wiring page</a>.</p>
</details>
